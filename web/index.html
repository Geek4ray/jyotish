How a reading is made

01Compute

Swiss Ephemeris positions, the lagna, the vargas and nakshatras, at your recorded time and at 200 times within your accuracy class.

02Measure

Dignity, lordship, aspects, Shadbala, Ashtakavarga, Bhava Bala, Jaimini karakas and arudhas, KP sub-lords.

03Score

Cited rules run on the chart, and seven signal families vote on each life area; the tier says how many of them agree.

04Narrate

The writer sees only the engine's findings, cites one for every sentence, and a second reader checks each sentence against the data.

Goal and honest scope

What the engine will not claim

What the engine will not claim: "precise" prediction of anything about anyone. No system has demonstrated that, and an app that promises it will be wrong in public. The product promise is: every classical signal, computed exactly, weighed transparently, with a confidence you can audit. The validation layer is what lets the promise grow only as far as the evidence does.

The precision ceiling is fixed by three things no engine removes: birth-time error (4 minutes moves D60 by a sign; most recorded times are rounded to 5–15 minutes), the texts disagreeing with each other across schools and recensions, and the absence of a demonstrated empirical signal. So the engine is precise in computation and reproducibility, and reports every finding with its stability under those three uncertainties. It is never precise about outcomes, and the product wording enforces that.

Excluded by design, so nobody wonders later: Prashna (horary), Muhurta (electional), Lal Kitab, Western tropical techniques, Bhrigu-leaf Nadi readings, and any death timing.

No life area is calibrated yet, so no reading uses probability language: each area shows "not calibrated" where a probability would go, and the tier words (strong, moderate, weak / mixed) are the engine's count of agreeing signal families, nothing more.
