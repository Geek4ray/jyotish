## Part 1 — Findings, most serious first

**F1. Test 4 (timing as survival) is nearly saturated: the engine's "activation" covers 87–96% of all
person-years, so the hazard test has almost nothing to compare against.**
`src/jyotish/cohort.py:30-37` marks a Vimshottari antar active when its lord *or any planet in its Nadi
activation set* rules, occupies or aspects any of the area's three or four houses or is one of its three
or four karakas; `scripts/validate.py:325-392` then reads that as the time-varying covariate (a year is
active when half of it is covered, `scripts/validate.py:337`). On the synthetic cohort the active share
is 0.871–0.956 for every area (`docs/validation_synthetic_planted_2000.md:57-69`). SPEC.md:312 asks for
"the engine's window activation", and SPEC.md:293 records why the timing join became v2.1: v2's
transit term sat at 1.0 in 33 of 37 slices and ranked nothing. The hazard test has rebuilt that
saturation with the dasha term alone (the transit term "left out for cost", `scripts/validate.py:24-27`).
A planted hazard ratio of about 2 (`scripts/synthetic_cohort.py:11-13, 129-132`) was recovered in 3 of 8
areas at N = 2,000 and only because the planted ages were drawn from the very same `active` list. On a
real cohort this test will report rate ratios near 1 whatever the engine does, and the prospective
record SPEC Layer 6b rests on (SPEC.md:341) would be scored with an instrument that cannot see.

**F2. The sequestered split is not group-aware, and its frozen list is one gitignored file whose loss
is silently a redraw.**
`scripts/validate.py:116-137` assigns by a salted hash of the subject id alone. Household and
birthplace groups guard only the development K-fold (`scripts/validate.py:140-180`), so a household's
charts can sit on both sides of the sequestered line, which SPEC.md:309 forbids ("related charts never
straddle a fold"; the sequestered line is the fold that matters most). The salt and every assignment
live in `data/sequestered.txt`, gitignored and on one machine; if the file is absent `sequester()` draws
a fresh salt (`scripts/validate.py:120-127`) with no warning. Its SHA-256 goes into the pre-registration
hashes (`scripts/validate.py:447-460`) but nothing compares it with a published value, and
`--score-sequestered` (`scripts/validate.py:463, 512-514`) leaves no record of how many times the
sequestered cohort has been scored, so "once per numbered release" (SPEC.md:309) is a convention, not a
check. The commit message for ee4a6d3 says "frozen the first time it runs; … nothing is redrawn", which
holds only while that one file survives.

**F3. SPEC test 8 (school-split) is not built, but the commit message, CLAUDE.md:158 and SPEC.md:345 all
say "tests 1-8 and 10".**
`scripts/validate.py:35-36` numbers the power table as "8"; SPEC.md:316 is the school-split (Parashari,
Jaimini, KP and Nadi families compared on development folds, the winner estimated once on the
sequestered cohort), and the power table is part of the outcome-dataset pre-registration (SPEC.md:305),
not a test. Nothing in validate.py splits by school. Test 9 (decoy) is also absent, which is expected
and not claimed. So the honest count is tests 1–7 and 10, with 4 as F1 describes it.

**F4. A subject's chart id is the engine's cache key, which contains the engine hash and the accuracy
class; the outcome capture breaks on the first engine change and erasure is incomplete.**
`src/jyotish/outcomes.py:152-158` keys a subject by `cache.chart_key`, and `src/jyotish/cache.py:40-44`
mixes `engine_hash()` (every source, data and rule file) and the `assess` kwargs (accuracy class, seed)
into it. Consequences: (a) after any engine change, a later sheet row for an existing subject computes
a different id and is refused as "the birth data differs from this subject's earlier rows"
(`src/jyotish/outcomes.py:189-191`); so does a corrected accuracy class. (b) `erase`
(`src/jyotish/outcomes.py:233-255`) deletes charts, readings, reviews and exclusions by that one chart
id, so a reading of the same birth made under a later engine hash survives the DPDP erasure the
commit message promises. (c) Twins, or two subjects with the same birth data, share a chart id, and
erasing one deletes the other's chart and readings. `tests/test_outcomes.py:102` tests erasure within
one engine hash only. The commit message's "the subject's chart id is the engine's chart key, so a
reading of the same birth data is found and erased with it" is true for one engine version only.

**F5. The CI gate treats noisy, self-referential statistics as monotone quality numbers, and the
scorecard states a falsehood about credentials.**
`scripts/evaluate.py:199-212` marks `synthetic.*.recovered_share`, `survived_bh_share` and
`timing_recovered_share` "higher is better". The planted events are drawn from the engine's own area
scores (`scripts/synthetic_cohort.py:86-100`), so any change to scoring redraws them even under the
fixed salt and seed; bcdcbfa's and 3c71259's messages record 0.50 against 0.75 recovery and 0.12 against
0.375 BH survival from the salt alone. Eight planted areas means one area is 0.125 of the share. A
legitimate scoring change will fail CI on noise, and a regression that happens to raise recovery will
pass. `metamorphic.seed_agreement.*` (0.9912–1.0, `scripts/evaluate.py:136`) and
`checker.validator.recall` (`scripts/evaluate.py:147`; it falls whenever a semantic row is added to the
benchmark) have the same problem. `scripts/evaluate.py:156-157` writes "no Anthropic credentials here"
unconditionally, so `docs/scorecard.md:102` says the live checker is blocked while
`docs/checker_scorecard.md:147-149`, committed later, has the run; the live checker's numbers are not in
the scorecard and not gated. `scripts/evaluate.py:196` writes the synthetic reports into `docs/`
whatever `--out` says, so running the gate locally dirties the working tree.

**F6. A chain finding's stability ignores the nakshatra boundaries its nakshatra chain depends on.**
`src/jyotish/chains.py:189-195` takes the smallest time-share of the D1 and D9 placements of the planets
the chains pass through and of the lagna's sign; the docstring (`src/jyotish/chains.py:19-22`) says the
nakshatra chain's own boundary "is not in that number". The lagna moves about 30′ across the ±2-minute
window and the Moon about 1′; on the reference chart Jupiter is 38.7′ from a nakshatra edge and the
lagna 230′, so nothing flips here, but on a chart whose lagna is within 30′ of a nakshatra edge the
`chain:personality.chart` finding (which starts the lagna's chain at `asc_nak_lord`,
`src/jyotish/chains.py:229-237`) and any chain through a planet near an edge would be stamped stable

**F7. The 88th-pada point has neither a source nor a structural reason, while the classical companion
of the 64th navamsa, the 22nd drekkana, is not computed; and its label contradicts the engine's own
tara table.**
`src/jyotish/nakpoints.py:5-8, 27-28` compute the 88th pada because "no source we hold defines an 88th
degree" (SPEC.md:125). The arithmetic says what the 88th should have been: the 64th navamsa is 63
navamsas on, 63 × 3°20′ = 210°; the 22nd drekkana is 21 drekkanas on, 21 × 10° = 210°; both land in
the eighth sign from the reference point, which is why the texts pair them as the Khara points
(`src/jyotish/nakpoints.py:24-25` even names the 22nd drekkana in a note). The 88th pada is 87 × 3°20′ =
290° on, the eleventh sign, and corresponds to nothing. SPEC's "88th-degree" is almost certainly a slip
for the 22nd drekkana. Separately, `src/jyotish/nakpoints.py:6` and SPEC.md:125 call the 22nd nakshatra
"the Vainashika", but `src/jyotish/constants.py:166-167` puts vinasha at 23 and SPEC.md:564 says "23rd
(Vinasha)"; the parenthetical is wrong by the project's own table.

**F8. The nakshatra attribute table's gender column agrees with neither of the two listings it could
be following.**
`src/jyotish/constants.py:116-145`, sixth column. Against the yoni sexes (the koota pairs, which are
structural: each animal has one male and one female nakshatra) it differs at Rohini (121: female; the
serpent pair makes Rohini male and Mrigashira female), Vishakha (133: female; male tiger), Shravana
(139: male; female monkey) and Uttara Bhadrapada (143: male; female cow). Against the modern
"nakshatra gender" listing the source note names (`src/jyotish/constants.py:112-114`) it differs at
about a dozen rows (Bharani, Punarvasu, Ashlesha, Magha, Uttara Phalguni, Hasta, Swati, Anuradha,
Jyeshtha, Mula, Purva and Uttara Ashadha). The other seven columns check against the texts I know:
deity, gana, yoni, nadi and activity (dhruva/chara/ugra/mishra/kshipra/mridu/tikshna) are all right;
tri-nadi is nadi under another name, as the comment says; direction (108-111) follows the
seven-to-a-quarter-from-Krittika scheme, and the other common scheme (south, west, north, east

chart page and not yet spoken.

**F9. The checker benchmark cannot measure the checker's false-reject rate, and the two
refers_to_another_claim rows are style faults the checker is built not to see.**
Every accepted row is a sentence of `examples/reference_reading_curious.json`
(`scripts/checker_bench.py:12-13, 193-208`), that is, a sentence the same checker accepted when the
reading was made. Precision 1.000 on the 45 accepted rows is therefore survivorship, not evidence; the
sentences the checker wrongly rejected on record ("slightly", "modest", SPEC.md:675) are exactly the
true-but-rejected cases a precision figure needs, and none is in the file. Of the 74 rejects, the 65
constructed rows and known_bad-3 to -9 are really wrong (checked one by one in Part 3). known_bad-1 and
-2 (`docs/checker_scorecard.md:140-142, 172-174`) are not false: every proposition in "The same
conjunction joins the Sun as 4th lord with Mercury …" and "… again forms Ruchaka yoga, with the same
weakening …" is supported by the finding's derivation, and the checker said so. Their fault is a
cross-reference to a claim the checker is given no sight of (SPEC.md:370: "given the sentence and the
finding's derivation only"), so recall is capped at 72/74 by design, not by a bad run. A validator rule
would catch both: `\bagain\b` anywhere, and "The same" at the start of a claim, reject known_bad-1 and
-2 and none of the 45 accepted rows (accept-15 has "in the same sign" mid-sentence, the only "same" in
the set). SPEC.md:370 declined a rule because "also" is often legitimate; this narrower rule is not
exposed to that. The live numbers (103 calls, 54 s, `docs/checker_scorecard.md:149`) are one sample of a
checker the SPEC itself calls a sample (SPEC.md:370); no repeat, no interval.

**F12. The synthetic runs understate the correlated-areas effect and never count false positives in
planted mode.**

gains (1.090, 0.0012) and reputation (1.043, 0.014), `docs/validation_synthetic_planted_20000.md:28, 31,
