## Findings, most severe first

### Q1 — high — The client's summary says the period to avoid is one the engine scores as favourable

- **Where:**
  - `src/jyotish/narrate.py:2417-2446` (`client_summary`): the windows sentence, then `parts.append(note)`;
  - `narrate.py:1242` (`timing_note`);
  - `tests/test_client_summary.py:43` (the test asserts the text ends with the note).
- **What is wrong:** for a timed area, the client's text names the strongest period and then the period to avoid, in
  one sentence. The next sentence is the timing note, which begins "These are periods the engine scores as favourable
  for …". "These" takes in the period to avoid. The engine scores that period as its least favourable.
- **Failure scenario (in a committed reading):** `docs/comparison_2026-10-31/b.reading.json`, `summary.client_text`:

  > The strongest period for career runs from October 2029 to June 2032; the period to avoid runs from November 2028 to
  > August 2029. These are periods the engine scores as favourable for career; it does not predict when you will act.

  The same text goes to every client whose summary falls back to the engine on a "when" question with an avoid window.
  Two of the four 2026-10-31 readings at astrologer depth fell back (fly-3, comparison B).
- **Also:**
  - "the period to avoid" reads to a client as advice; the field is the engine's lowest-scored window.
  - The contradiction predates the batch on the model summary's face, where "When (engine): … Window to avoid: …" is
    followed by the same note. The batch puts it into the client's own words.
- **What a fix needs:**
  - A note that describes only what it follows: the favourable period, and the low-scored period apart, worded as the
    engine's score rather than a recommendation.
  - A test that reads what the client's sentences say, not only their vocabulary (see Q11, DoD 5).

### Q3 — medium — A paragraph held sentence by sentence is never read as a whole

- **Where:** `src/jyotish/live.py:195-216` (`HeldParagraph.key`, `fresh`, `place`); `narrate.py:685` (`CROSS_REFERENCE`
  refused only when `standalone`); `narrate.py:2559` (`_summary_checks`: each sentence checked alone).
- **What is wrong:** the held paragraph is the concatenation of sentences from different attempts, each validated and
  checked alone. Nothing reads the result.
  - **Position.** A retry's j-th sentence takes the j-th open key. A rewrite of failed sentence 1 written as two
    sentences, with sentence 3 also open, puts its second half at key 3 and the rewrite of 3 at "3.1". Reproduced:
    "A one, first half. B two. A one, second half. C again. D four."
  - **Repeats.** `fresh` drops only verbatim repeats of held sentences. A writer that rewrites the whole paragraph in
    other words places its paraphrases as extra sentences. Reproduced: "A one. A one again in other words. B fixed.
    C three, reworded. C three."
  - **Seen in a committed reading** (`docs/comparison_2026-10-31/a.reading.json`, career). Sentence 1 (attempt 1) says
    "Sun and Mercury conjoin in the 10th, forming Budhaditya yoga and Kendra‑Trikona Raja yoga". The rewritten
    sentence 2 (attempt 2) opens "Sun and Mercury conjoin in the 10th, forming Kendra‑Trikona Raja yoga, …". The
    client reads the conjunction twice.
  - **References across sentences.**
    - The validator reads paragraph sentences with `standalone=False`, because "a summary or a paragraph is read whole
      and may" refer to another sentence. Claims, which are checked alone, refuse "again", "as noted", "this same".
    - A paragraph sentence is now held alone, yet keeps the paragraph's licence.
    - The checker sees each sentence with only the findings its tags cite. So "It is in its own sign [vote:…]" can pass
      about one planet and stand after a sentence about another, or at the paragraph's head once sentence 1 is refused.
  - **Shape.** At depth full the client's paragraph is "the answer …, then what supports it, then what works against
    it" (`client.ClientArea`). If the answer sentence never passes, "What the chart says for you" is the for and
    against sentences alone.
- **What a fix needs:**
  - The writer returns each rewrite with the position it replaces, and extra sentences are refused or placed only
    after their own failed sentence.
  - A reworded repeat of a held sentence is not placed.
  - Either `CROSS_REFERENCE` (and pronoun openers) is refused on a held paragraph sentence, or the composed paragraph
    gets one checker call as a synthesis.
  - A paragraph whose first sentence never passed is a gap.
  - Tests for each.

### Q8 — low — `_names_lordship` reads a list where the sentence states none, and misses some lists it states

- **False accepts (each refused before 1ec91cd, accepted now):**

    - "The Sun sits in the 10th and the 5th lord Mercury joins it [rule:place.l10_h10]";
    - "Venus sits in the 7th and the 12th lord aspects it [rule:place.l7_h7]".

  - "the lord of both the 2nd and the 5th";
  - "rules both the 2nd and the 5th";
  - "rules over the 2nd and 5th";
  - "the 2nd and 5th houses' lord";
  - "lord of the 2nd as well as the 5th";
  - "lord of the second and fifth houses".
- **Coverage:**
  - The benchmark has the accept row only. The three refused sentences are in `test_narrate.py`, not in
    `checker_bench.jsonl`, so the validator's false-accept rate on lists is not measured.
  - The checker reads the proposition next, so the effect is on the code gate, hence low.
- **What a fix needs:**
  - A list that starts after "in the" / "sits in the" is not a lordship list.
  - No `the` after a joiner unless the next word is an ordinal followed by a lordship word or the list's end.
